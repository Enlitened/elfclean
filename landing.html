Find the space AI and code left behind

Building with AI, or just running it? Coding agents, local models and AI apps quietly fill your Mac. ElfClean finds that space, explains each item in plain language and moves nothing until you've reviewed it.

Download on the Mac App Store Scanning is free. macOS 15 or later.

Who it's for

Vibe coders

Every project an AI helped you build keeps its own node_modules, build folders and caches, and coding agents leave worktrees, logs and old versions behind. ElfClean finds them in projects you haven't touched for a while, and tells you which projects aren't saved anywhere else before you remove anything.

People who run AI on their Mac

Local models are often 5 to 40 GB each. See every Ollama and Hugging Face model, with shared files counted once, add model folders you keep elsewhere, and clear AI apps' caches and logs, in plain language.

Developers

Xcode DerivedData and simulators, package caches for every major ecosystem, and the Git worktrees your agents made, with the merged ones ready to prune.

What it finds

Xcode and simulators

DerivedData, archives, device support and simulator devices, with the runtime each device uses.

Project build folders

node_modules, .next, .build, Rust target, uv .venv and Pods in projects you haven't touched for a while. Recent projects start unticked; Full Clean includes them only after its warning and review confirmation.

Package caches

npm, pnpm, Yarn, Bun, pip, uv, Poetry, Cargo, Go, Gradle, CocoaPods, Swift packages and Homebrew.

AI models and agents

Ollama and Hugging Face models, shared layers counted once. Caches, logs, old versions and history from Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Devin, VS Code, Roo Code, Aider and Continue, and the Git worktrees agents leave behind. History is archived, never treated as a cache.

Everyday clutter

App caches and logs, old downloads, browser caches and leftovers from apps you deleted, in plain language.

Where space went

A storage map of your home folder, large files with Quick Look, and duplicates found byte for byte.

Careful by design

  • You review every item. Nothing moves until you've seen what it is, what removing it changes and how to get it back.
  • The Trash first. Items go to the Trash by default; History records where each came from and can put it back while it is still there. Space is freed when you empty the Trash.
  • Git-aware. A project with work that isn't committed or pushed anywhere is marked At risk and never ticked for you, and is re-checked just before removal. Handy when an AI wrote most of the code and you're not sure what's saved.
  • Hands off. Mail, Keychains, Photos, iCloud Drive, cloud drives and anything outside your home folder are never touched.
  • Honest numbers. No "speeds up your Mac", no health score: just measured sizes.
